About Me

Hello! I'm Dante Buhl, a PhD student in Applied Mathematics at the University of California, Santa Cruz, advised by Professor Pascale Garaud. My research focuses on stratified turbulence, rotating flows, and high-performance computing applied to geophysical and astrophysical fluid dynamics.

I received my M.S. in Scientific Computing and Applied Mathematics from UCSC in August 2024, with a thesis on the effect of rotation on stratified turbulence. Before that, I completed my B.A. in Mathematics with highest honors from UCSC in June 2023.

Prior Research Projects

Simulating Spherical Ciliated Organism Locomotion Summer 2023

Program: Towson University REU  ·  Advisor: Dr. Hervé Nganguia

Applied machine-learning methods (DeepXDE) to solve the Navier-Stokes equations in Stokes flow, Brinkman flow, and shear-thinning media with spherical boundary conditions, modeling the propulsion of ciliated microorganisms. Results were presented at the NSA Summer REU Conference and the Towson University REU symposium.

Fluid Dynamics Machine Learning Stokes Flow DeepXDE Python
Boxes and Balls: Quantifying Chaos Winter 2023

Program: Directed Reading Program, UCSC Mathematics Dept.  ·  Mentor: John G. Pelias

Developed MATLAB code to numerically compute multiple definitions of fractal dimension (box-counting, Lyapunov, Renyi, pointwise) for chaotic attractors, including the Lorenz attractor. Topics covered included the Variational Equation, Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization, and numerical computation of Lyapunov exponents. Culminated in a 15-minute departmental presentation.

Dynamical Systems Fractal Dimension Lyapunov Exponents MATLAB
The Pow-der of Ani-snow-tropy Fall 2022

Course: AM 214: Applied Dynamical Systems, UC Santa Cruz

Group analysis (with Hayley Coyle and Sebastian Osorio) of the 6-variable Lorenz Ski-Slope system from Edward Lorenz's The Essence of Chaos, examining chaotic behavior and parameter sensitivity.

Dynamical Systems Lorenz System Chaos Theory
