About Me

Hello! I'm Dante Buhl, a PhD student in Applied Mathematics at the University of California, Santa Cruz, advised by Professor Pascale Garaud (expected 2028). My research focuses on rotating stratified turbulence in geophysical and astrophysical flows. I build and run large MPI-parallel Fortran simulations on high-performance computing clusters and analyze the resulting datasets to extract scaling laws for turbulent transport and mixing.

I received my M.S. in Scientific Computing and Applied Mathematics from UCSC in August 2024, with a thesis on the effect of rotation on stratified turbulence. Before that, I completed my B.A. in Mathematics (Computational Mathematics) with highest honors from UCSC in June 2023.

Independent Projects

Statistical Arbitrage via Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Mean Reversion Fall 2026

Type: Independent study in stochastic methods for quantitative finance

  • Modeling spreads of cointegrated asset pairs as Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes; deriving the exact discretization and maximum-likelihood estimators for the mean-reversion rate, long-run mean, and volatility.
  • Assessing numerical and statistical accuracy: strong convergence of Euler–Maruyama against exact simulation, and small-sample bias in mean-reversion estimates.
  • Selecting pairs with Engle–Granger cointegration tests and multiple-testing controls; estimating time-varying hedge ratios with a Kalman filter.
  • Evaluating z-score trading rules strictly out of sample with transaction costs, including analysis of periods where pair relationships break down.
Stochastic Processes Quantitative Finance Kalman Filter Python

Simulating Spherical Ciliated Organism Locomotion Summer 2023

Program: Towson University REU  ·  Advisor: Dr. Hervé Nganguia

Applied machine-learning methods (DeepXDE) to solve the Navier-Stokes equations in Stokes flow, Brinkman flow, and shear-thinning media with spherical boundary conditions, modeling the propulsion of ciliated microorganisms. Boxes and Balls: Quantifying Chaos Winter 2023

Program: Directed Reading Program, UCSC Mathematics Dept.  ·  Mentor: Dr. John G. Pelias

Developed MATLAB code to numerically compute multiple definitions of fractal dimension (box-counting, Lyapunov, Renyi, pointwise) for chaotic attractors, including the Lorenz attractor. Topics covered included the Variational Equation, Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization, and numerical computation of Lyapunov exponents. Culminated in a 15-minute departmental presentation.

Dynamical Systems Fractal Dimension Lyapunov Exponents MATLAB
The Pow-der of Ani-snow-tropy Fall 2022

Type: Graduate course research project  ·  Course: AM 214: Applied Dynamical Systems (graduate), UC Santa Cruz

Group analysis (with Hayley Coyle and Sebastian Osorio) of the 6-variable Lorenz Ski-Slope system from Edward Lorenz's The Essence of Chaos, examining chaotic behavior and parameter sensitivity.
