Rotating Stratified Turbulence

PhD research, UC Santa Cruz  ·  Advisor: Pascale Garaud  ·  2023 – Present

Stratified Turbulence DNS Fortran / MPI

My PhD research at UC Santa Cruz, advised by Professor Pascale Garaud, focuses on rotating, stably stratified turbulence in geophysical and astrophysical flows. Stratification suppresses vertical motion and organizes the flow into thin, layered structures, while rotation further modifies how energy is transferred between scales and how efficiently the fluid mixes.

I build and run direct numerical simulations of these flows using MPI-parallel Fortran codes on high-performance computing clusters, and analyze the resulting datasets to extract scaling laws for turbulent transport and mixing. This work began with my M.S. thesis, The Effect of Rotation on Stratified Turbulence, and has been presented at APS DFD 2024 and EGU 2025.

Some details of the work on this project

  • Direct numerical simulation of the Boussinesq equations
  • Multiscale models of nonrotating stratified turbulence
  • Mixing models and measurements of turbulent transport
  • MPI-parallel Fortran on HPC clusters
  • Large-scale data analysis in Python
